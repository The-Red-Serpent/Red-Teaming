## Core Search Operators

- `site:` — Searches for information only within a specific website or domain.

- `filetype:` / `ext:` — Helps you find files in a specific format, such as PDF, DOCX, XLSX, or PPTX.

- `intitle:` — Finds webpages that contain a specific word or phrase in their title.

- `allintitle:` — Finds webpages whose titles contain all the words you specify.

- `inurl:` — Finds webpages with a specific word or term in their web address (URL).

- `allinurl:` — Looks for webpages whose URLs contain all the specified words.

- `intext:` — Searches for a specific word or phrase within a webpage's text.

- `allintext:` — Looks for webpages whose text contains all the words you specify.

- `cache:` — Was used to access Google's saved copy of a webpage. Google has discontinued its public cache feature, so this operator is no longer useful for that purpose.

## Modifiers, Symbols, and Logic

- `" "` (Quotation marks) — Searches for an exact phrase, keeping the words in the same order.

- `-` (Minus sign) — Excludes a particular word or website from your search results.

- `OR` — Finds results containing either one search term or another.

- `AND` — Indicates that both terms are relevant to your search. Google usually treats spaces between words similarly, so using `AND` is often unnecessary.

- `*` (Asterisk) — Acts as a wildcard that can stand in for an unknown word within a phrase.

- `before:` — Filters search results to dates before a specified date.

- `after:` — Filters search results to dates after a specified date.

- `AROUND(n)` — Attempts to find two words or phrases that appear close to each other, within a specified number of words. Support may vary.

- `@` — Can be used in searches involving social media handles or mentions, although its usefulness depends on the search.
